# -ESG-Risk-Opportunity-Assessment-Tata-Steel-BRSR-# Tata Steel — ESG Risk & Opportunity Analysis
**BRSR FY2024-25 | Standalone & Consolidated**

A structured ESG analysis of Tata Steel Limited based on its officially filed Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report (BRSR) for FY2024-25, submitted to BSE and NSE (Ref: SEC/373/2025-26).

---

## What This Project Does

This project analyses Tata Steel's ESG performance across **7 dimensions** — Climate, Water, Safety, Social & Workforce, Circular Economy, Governance, and Community — using publicly disclosed BRSR data. It identifies material risks, positive trends, and gaps, and produces a prioritised recommendation framework for ESG analysts.

## Top 3 ESG Risks Identified

**1. Carbon transition risk — HIGH**

Scope 1 emissions rose to 61 MnT CO₂e (standalone, FY25). Renewable energy share remains critically low at 0.07%. No interim 2030 reduction target exists despite a Net Zero 2045 commitment — creating a credibility and regulatory exposure gap.

**2. Safety — high-consequence injuries — MEDIUM-HIGH**

Despite LTIFR improving from 0.51 → 0.39, standalone high-consequence worker injuries doubled from 9 to 18. The contract workforce of 1,55,229 non-permanent workers carries disproportionate risk exposure and lower visibility in reporting.

**3. Gender representation and pay gap — MEDIUM**

Female employees represent only 9.1% of the permanent workforce. Zero female KMPs at the most senior executive level. Gross wages paid to females are 6% of total wages (standalone). No adjusted pay equity audit has been disclosed.

---

## Top 3 Opportunities Identified

**1. Circular economy and scrap scaling**

4.2 MT of scrap recycled in FY25. Tata Steel UK's EAF transition will push scrap usage from 17% to over 70%. The Steel Recycling Business (SRB) and Industrial By-Products Management Division (IBMD) are scalable commercial platforms.

**2. Renewable energy transition**

Current renewable base is 0.07% — significant headroom to grow via Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) and rooftop solar. The Jamshedpur warehouse is already energy-positive (2,303 MWh generated vs 1,582 MWh consumed from a 2.2 MW rooftop solar plant).

**3. Water innovation and ZED expansion**

A pilot electrochemical reactor at Kalinganagar is achieving ~90% reduction in COD, turbidity, and TSS — enabling potential water savings of 250 m³/hr. ZED is already achieved at 2 of 4 major Indian plants and can be extended further.

## Priority Recommendations

| Priority | Pillar | Recommendation | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | E | Set 2030 SBTi-aligned interim GHG reduction targets to bridge the Net Zero 2045 credibility gap | 0–12 months |
| P1 | S | Investigate and publicly report root cause of doubling in high-consequence worker injuries | 0–6 months |
| P1 | S | Appoint at least one female KMP to close the 0% representation gap at executive level | 0–12 months |
| P2 | E | Accelerate renewable energy procurement — target 15–20% mix by FY2027 via PPAs | 12–24 months |
| P2 | E | Extend Zero Effluent Discharge to all 4 major Indian plants with site-level milestones | 12–36 months |
| P2 | S | Publish gender pay equity audit with adjusted gap; set 20% female employee target by 2030 | 12–18 months |
| P3 | G | Bring TSUK and TSN under consolidated BRSR reasonable assurance scope by FY2026-27 | 24–36 months |
| P3 | E | Initiate India Green Credit Programme participation at mining rehabilitation and water conservation sites | 12–24 months |
